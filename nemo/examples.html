Worked examples

Rare virulence and reproductive failure

p=nemo.Parameters(b=0)
small=nemo.run_simulation(p,nemo.Schedule('R',20),
  initial_density=80,virulence_frequency=.01,
  area_m2=.0054,n_replicates=2000,seed=42)
large=nemo.run_simulation(p,nemo.Schedule('R',20),
  initial_density=80,virulence_frequency=.2,
  area_m2=1.,n_replicates=2000,seed=42)
print(small.outcomes((5,10,20)))
print(large.outcomes((5,10,20)))

The scenarios do not isolate the effect of initial frequency because population area also changes. A proper factorial contrast fixes each factor in turn. Use female_recruitment_risk() to relate both factors to the expected initial number of reproductive females.

Annual suicide-hatching schedule

b = [.1,.8,.7] * 10
s = nemo.Schedule('RNN',30,biocontrol=b)
r = nemo.run_simulation(nemo.Parameters(),s,area_m2=.01,
                        n_replicates=2000,seed=8)
print(s.table().head(9))

Quantifying uncertainty around an intervention threshold

bs = [round(x,2) for x in np.arange(0,.951,.05)]
frame = nemo.compare_strategies(['RN','RNN','RNNN'],bs,[.05,.2,.6],
                                years=50,n_replicates=1000)
subset = frame[(frame.q0==.2)&(frame.year==50)]
print(subset[['cycle','b','p_below','p_sustained','p_extinct']])

Independent mating neighbourhoods

out = nemo.compare_isolated_patches(
    patch_counts=[1,2,4,8],area_m2=.05,
    schedule=nemo.Schedule('RNN',30),
    n_replicates=1000)

Each experiment should be reported with the population scale and assumptions that generated it. Different patch counts do not independently identify real migration processes.
