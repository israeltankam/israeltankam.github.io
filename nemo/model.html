Biological model

State and genetic structure

The population is characterised by three genotype counts, $N_{AA}$, $N_{Aa}$ and $N_{aa}$. A diallelic locus governs virulence towards a masculinizing resistance. The virulence allele $a$ is recessive; only $aa$ is virulent. The allele frequency and virulent-homozygote frequency are different quantities:

$$q=(N_{Aa}+2N_{aa})/(2N),\qquad v=N_{aa}/N.$$

The default starting proportions use Hardy–Weinberg, $((1-q)^2,2q(1-q),q^2)$, but users can give any nonnegative genotype composition. Hardy–Weinberg is an initial assumption, not an equilibrium identity under the resistance.

Recruitment and sex determination

Under establishment regulation, each entering juvenile establishes independently with probability $s/(1+\alpha n)$. On susceptible potato, an established individual becomes male with probability $m$ irrespective of genotype. On masculinizing-resistant potato, AA and Aa become male with probability 1, while aa retains the probability $m$.

Under the alternative fecundity-regulated model, establishment probability remains $s$ and eggs per female decrease as $e/(1+\alpha F/\kappa)$. The two assumptions affect the demographic mechanism and must not be substituted without calibration.

Genetic combinations

Let $(w_0,w_1,w_2)$ denote the genotype proportions among males. The probabilities for each offspring genotype, in the order (AA, Aa, aa), are:

$$P_{AA\text{ mother}}=(w_0+w_1/2,\quad w_2+w_1/2,\quad 0),$$

$$P_{Aa\text{ mother}}=(w_0/2+w_1/4,\quad (w_0+w_1+w_2)/2,\quad w_2/2+w_1/4),$$

$$P_{aa\text{ mother}}=(0,\quad w_0+w_1/2,\quad w_2+w_1/2).$$

Each female contributes according to its own genotype vector; the offspring probabilities are weighted by the numbers of mothers in the three genotype classes. These are direct genetic combinations without a Hardy–Weinberg assumption at later generations.

Eggs and survival

A mother generates a Poisson number of eggs with mean e or the regulated fecundity e_eff. Offspring genotypes are drawn independently from the corresponding combination probabilities. Each year, eggs survive with probability $(1-\mu)(1-h)(1-b_t)$. In a nonhost year, no new generation is produced; only egg survival occurs.

Scale and mating

The number of simulated individuals equals soil density times the mass of the assumed infested focus. The default conversion uses a soil layer of 0.20 m and density of 1.4 g/cm³. Mating is random within the selected focus. A large mapped infested area need not constitute one biological mating neighbourhood; users should treat this scale as a structural assumption.

Selected genetic equilibrium

For $0<m<1/2$ under continuous resistant cultivation, the internal deterministic equilibrium frequency of aa is $v=1/[2(1-m)]$. The corresponding allele frequency is $q^=(1+v)/2$.

See the article and supplementary material for exact equilibrium conditions and extinction results.
