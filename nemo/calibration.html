Estimating demographic and genetic parameters

Calibration must begin with the observation process. Pot-level egg counts, published means and standard errors, hatch counts, residual eggs in cysts, time series and population-genetic samples cannot share one universal likelihood.

Summary of available fitters

Function Primary observation schema Objective Main limitation
fit_recruitment_fecundity Pi_eggs_per_g, cysts_per_g, cysts_se, eggs_per_cyst, eggs_per_cyst_se Separate weighted recruitment and fecundity fits Summary statistics are not raw pot counts
fit_fecundity Pi_eggs_per_g, eggs_per_cyst, eggs_per_cyst_se Weighted constant or reciprocal fecundity Does not identify establishment
fit_hatching hatched, tested and optional exposure Binomial constant or exposure-dependent hatching Does not identify survival in soil
fit_egg_survival alive, tested, years Binomial cumulative annual survival Cannot separate natural mortality and hatching
fit_population_trajectory year, density and optional se Weighted deterministic trajectory Depends on initial condition and fixed parameters
fit_allele_trajectory year, a_copies, allele_copies Binomial genetic observation fit Does not include process-noise likelihood
fit_reproduction_factor Pi_eggs_per_g, Pf_eggs_per_g and optional Pf_se Weighted one-crop Pi/Pf response Identifiability depends on fixed biological assumptions

Greenhouse calibration compatible with the manuscript

The package includes the published summary data from Contina, Dandurand and Knudsen (2019), Plant Disease, doi:10.1094/PDIS-04-19-0717-RE. The trial 1 and pooled trials 2/3 are preserved separately. The observations concern initial eggs per g soil, cysts per g soil and eggs per cyst.

import pandas as pd
import nemo_pcn as nemo
obs = pd.read_csv('nemo-pcn/data/published_greenhouse_summary.csv')
group = obs[obs.trial=='trials_2_3']
fit = nemo.fit_recruitment_fecundity(
    group, hatch_viability=.45, m=.35,
    fecundity='constant',bootstrap=500,seed=12)
print(fit.estimates)
print(fit.diagnostics)
fit.export('outputs/greenhouse_fit')

The recruitment equation is:

\[C(P_i)=\frac{0.45 P_i s(1-m)}{1+\alpha(0.45 P_i)}.\]

The constant eggs-per-cyst observation model is $E(P_i)=e$, and the alternative reciprocal model is $E(P_i)=e_0/(1+\theta P_i)$. Recruitment parameters cannot generally be uniquely recovered from final eggs per g alone. Parameters that are held fixed must be recorded.

Fitting hatching assays

h = pd.DataFrame({'hatched':[18,37,54],
                  'tested':[100,100,100],
                  'exposure':[2,5,10]})
fit = nemo.fit_hatching(h,model='exponential')
print(fit.estimates)

This illustrates a binomial likelihood, not a claim that exposure time is the sole determinant of experimental hatch response.

Fitting genetic samples

genetic = pd.DataFrame({'year':[0,1,2,3],
                        'a_copies':[40,58,85,105],
                        'allele_copies':[200,200,200,200]})
fit = nemo.fit_allele_trajectory(genetic,cycle='R',fit=('m',))

This is a conditional sampling likelihood against deterministic trajectories. At low population sizes, state-space particle methods would be needed to jointly infer selection and process drift.

Identifiability and validation

A successful optimiser is not evidence that every biological parameter is identified. Inspect residuals, profile surfaces, alternative mortality decompositions and fitted predictions on omitted experimental levels. Transferability to a different population requires data collected under the target soil, cultivar and management context.
